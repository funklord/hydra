Tide pools: a field guide

8 minute read

Twice a day the sea steps back and leaves small worlds behind in the hollows of the rocks. A tide pool can hold more life in a square metre than the beach around it holds in a hundred.

When to go

Aim for an hour either side of low tide, on a calm day. Spring tides, a few days after a full or new moon, uncover the most.

Look, then put it back. Turn a stone over gently and return it the way it was: its underside is somebody's home.

What you might find

Starfish wedged in the cracks, anemones folded shut until the water returns, hermit crabs trading shells, and the quick grey shapes of blennies that vanish as your shadow falls across the pool.
